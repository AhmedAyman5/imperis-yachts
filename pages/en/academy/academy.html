Accredited Maritime Training

Trained Here, Certified By The Arab Academy

IMPERIS Academy works in partnership with the Arab Academy for Science, Technology and Maritime Transport — the region's established authority on maritime education. Our trainees are recruited through that pipeline and graduate through it, which means the certification they leave with is recognised across the Arab maritime sector rather than being a certificate that only means something inside our own yard.

That accreditation matters, but it isn't the point. The gap we set out to close is the one every shipyard in the region knows well: a graduate can arrive with a solid theoretical grounding in naval systems and still have never cut a plate, struck an arc, or traced a fault on a live vessel. Classroom knowledge tells you how a system should behave. It doesn't prepare you for the moment a weld doesn't take, a measurement disagrees with the drawing, or a fitting has to be reached through an access panel that looks nothing like the diagram.

So our trainees spend most of their time on the shop floor, not in a lecture room. They rotate through our own build, welding, systems, and finishing teams, working on vessels that are genuinely under contract — with real deadlines, real inspections, and real consequences for getting it wrong. They're taught by the engineers and craftsmen who build our yachts, which means they learn our standards from the people who set them. By the time a trainee finishes, they've done the work rather than studied it, and they can walk onto any yard in the region and be useful on the first day.

Who We Are

Knowledge, Passed On Where It's Built

IMPERIS Academy transfers the technical and industrial knowledge of yacht and marine vessel construction to the next generation of engineers and technicians — combining theoretical training with hands-on practice, real industrial environment exposure, and quality & safety procedures.

Most technical education stops at theory. IMPERIS Academy runs inside a working 5,490 m² shipyard, so trainees see how a hull is laminated, a system is wired or a joint is welded on the same floor where real vessels are built — not in a simulated classroom. Students, graduates, working technicians and whole corporate teams learn beside the specialists who build and repair vessels every day.

The Commercial Difference

Built To A Different Brief

A commercial vessel is judged by a harder standard than a private yacht. It has to leave the berth on schedule every day of the season, absorb heavy use from crews who are working rather than relaxing, and come back for maintenance without taking a revenue week out of the operator's year. That changes almost every decision we make: material thickness, deck surfaces, access panels, equipment placement, and how easily a worn component can be reached and replaced at three in the morning.

We build these vessels in the same yard, to the same structural discipline, as our yachts — but with the finishing priorities inverted. Durability comes first, ease of maintenance second, and everything cosmetic is chosen for how well it survives a decade of commercial service. Operators along the Red Sea and the Gulf of Suez don't need a vessel that photographs well on delivery day; they need one that's still earning in year eight.

Shipyard Capability

One Yard, Every Stage

Nothing on a commercial order is farmed out to a subcontractor whose schedule we don't control. Hull fabrication, structural welding, systems installation, and fit-out all happen inside our own facility on the Gulf of Suez, which is the only reason we can give an operator a delivery date and stand behind it. When a build slips at a yard that outsources half its work, nobody can say exactly where the delay came from. Here, the naval architects who drew the general arrangement can walk out to the shop floor and look at the plate.

Operating Life & Support

A Vessel That Earns Needs A Yard That Answers

For a commercial operator, a breakdown isn't an inconvenience — it's lost revenue with a date attached. That's why our refit and maintenance division treats commercial clients differently from private owners: scheduled servicing planned around the operating season rather than against it, emergency work prioritised by how much the downtime actually costs, and a parts and drawings record kept for every vessel we deliver so nothing has to be reverse-engineered years later. We also take on refits and conversions of vessels we didn't build, because a hull that's structurally sound is often worth far more rebuilt than replaced.

Why We Keep It In-House

Control Is a Design Decision

Most yards assemble vessels from other people's work. We chose the harder route — building the capability to do every core discipline ourselves — because it changes what we can promise an owner.

01

Faster Iteration

When an owner changes a layout or an engineer refines a detail, the revision moves from the drawing office to the workshop floor in days, not weeks. Engineering, fabrication and installation share one roof — there is no subcontractor queue to join and no second contract to renegotiate.

02

One Quality Standard

Every weld, every laminate and every circuit is judged against the same internal standard, from the first cut of material to final commissioning. There is no hand-off where one supplier's "acceptable" quietly becomes the next one's starting point.

03

Full Accountability

One shipyard, one team and one point of contact carry the owner from first concept through delivery and into after-sales. When a question comes up in year five, the people answering it are the people who built the vessel.

Our Quality Approach

Every Discipline, One Quality Chain

Nothing on this page works in isolation. Composites, metalwork, power, electrical and systems all feed into the same quality chain, and every stage has to pass before the next one begins.

  1. Incoming Material Inspection

    Resin, plate, cable and components are checked against specification before they enter production.

  2. In-Process Checks

    Laminates, welds and installations are inspected at defined hold points throughout the build.

  3. Full Testing Before Sign-Off

    Systems are pressure-, load- and function-tested, then commissioned before the vessel is signed off.
